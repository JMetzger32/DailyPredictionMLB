# Joint moneyline + run-line edge — does adding the spread market help?

Generated by `scripts/joint_edge_research.py` and `scripts/segment_joint_edge.py`.
Walk-forward OOF model probabilities (`oof_predictions` + the new
`oof_runline_predictions`) joined to de-vigged archived market prices from
`odds_snapshots` (h2h) and the new `odds_snapshots_spreads` (run line).
n = 10,565 joined games; 8,233 scored by the walk-forward second stage
(seasons 2023-2026; 2022 is consumed as the first training fold).

## What was built to make the comparison possible

The run-line market quotes -1.5 on whichever side is favoured, so the spreads
table's `home_implied` is P(home covers **its own** line): P(margin > 1.5) when home
lays -1.5, but P(margin > -1.5) when home takes +1.5. Those are different events, and
the shipped run-line model only predicted the first one — `away_cover_prob` is
`1 - home_cover_prob`, i.e. "home fails to win by 2+", which also counts one-run games
either way and is **not** "away wins by 2+".

So `scripts/train_away_runline.py` added an away-side model (`away_covers =
(visitor - home) > 1.5`), same architecture and hyperparameters as the home side,
stored as three new pkl keys (`lr_runline_away`, `gb_runline_away`,
`scaler_runline_away`). `model_version` is unchanged (`24c1e93ac246`) because
`compute_model_version` hashes only the moneyline LR + scaler — no served moneyline
probability moved. With both sides available, model and market are compared on the
identical event for **every** game:

| market's line | market prices | model prices |
|---|---|---|
| home lays -1.5 | `home_implied` = P(margin > 1.5) | `home_cover_prob` |
| home takes +1.5 | `home_implied` = P(margin > -1.5) | `1 - away_by2_prob` |

6,132 games of the first kind, 4,433 of the second. No games dropped, no threshold
mismatch.

## 1. Each market beats the model on its own event

| | model AUC | market AUC |
|---|---|---|
| moneyline (home win) | 0.5740 | **0.6032** |
| run line (home covers its line) | 0.5924 | **0.5958** |

Consistent with CLAUDE.md's existing moneyline finding. Notably the run-line model is
*closer* to its market (−0.003) than the moneyline model is to its (−0.029) — but it
still does not beat it.

## 2. The run-line terms add nothing detectable to the moneyline picture

`logit(home_win) ~ model_ml + market_ml` vs the same plus `model_rl + market_rl`:

```
baseline      model_ml +0.0556 (p=0.471)   market_ml +0.9634 (p=0.000)
+ run line    model_ml -0.0327 (p=0.736)   market_ml +1.0739 (p=0.000)
              model_rl +0.1035 (p=0.135)   market_rl -0.0057 (p=0.953)

LR test, 2 added terms:  chi2=4.429  p=0.1092   -> NOT significant
```

`market_rl` is dead on arrival (p=0.95): the moneyline market already contains
whatever the run-line market knows. `model_rl` is the only term that comes close
(p=0.135) and it does not clear the bar. The baseline's `model_ml` p=0.471 reproduces
the known result in `feature_gap_vs_market.md` (p=0.4625), which is a useful check
that this pipeline agrees with the existing one.

## 3. The second stage mostly learns to copy the market

Walk-forward second-stage LR on the four logits, each season scored by a fit that
never saw it:

```
AUC   old model_ml 0.5767   market 0.5946   second-stage 0.5944
mean |edge|   old 0.0518   new 0.0087       (a ~6x collapse in scale)
```

The second stage lands on the market's AUC almost exactly, because the honest thing
to do with a model that adds nothing is to defer to the price. That is also why the
edge shrinks ~6x: `second_stage_prob ≈ market_prob`, so the difference is small.

## 4. But the new edge is no longer ANTI-predictive — the one real improvement

`logit(bet won) ~ |edge|`, same 8,233 games:

| edge | slope | p | flat win% |
|---|---|---|---|
| old (ml only) | **−0.98** | 0.079 | 47.4% |
| new (ml+rl) | **+4.87** | 0.125 | **49.8%** |

The old edge's slope is *negative* — bigger claimed edge, worse results — matching
the "edge is ANTI-predictive" finding already in this repo. The new edge flips the
sign and gains 2.4pp of win rate, and holds up in 3 of 4 seasons:

```
season      n   old win%   new win%
  2023   2344      47.0%      50.2%
  2024   2420      47.3%      50.3%
  2025   2421      47.6%      49.6%
  2026   1048      48.3%      47.8%   <- the one season the old edge wins
```

Best single cell in the whole sweep: new edge at threshold 0.010 →
**51.8% [50.1, 53.5] on 2,938 bets**, a CI that excludes 50%.

## 5. It still does not clear the vig — state this plainly

Every ROI above is priced at the **de-vigged** market probability, i.e. with the
bookmaker's hold removed. Real prices are ~4-5pp worse, and breakeven at a standard
-110 is **52.4%**. The best measured cell is 51.8% with a CI of [50.1, 53.5] — it
straddles breakeven rather than clearing it. Flat ROI at every threshold is within
noise of zero:

```
new edge  thresh 0.000  n=8233  49.8%  ROI  -0.1% [-2.4, 2.1]
          thresh 0.010  n=2938  51.8%  ROI  +0.9% [-2.8, 4.7]
          thresh 0.020  n= 593  51.6%  ROI  +0.6% [-7.4, 7.8]
```

**Conclusion:** adding the spread market does not produce a demonstrably profitable
betting signal. What it does produce is an edge metric that is no longer actively
misleading — it stops pointing the wrong way. That is a real improvement over the
shipped metric, and it is not the same thing as an edge.

## 6. Product consequence — thresholds MUST be recalibrated

The new edge lives on a ~6x smaller scale, so the shipped 0.05 / 0.12 bars are
meaningless against it:

| | \|edge\| ≥ 0.05 | \|edge\| ≥ 0.12 |
|---|---|---|
| old edge | 3,590 games (43.6%) | 553 (6.7%) |
| new edge | **3 games (0.0%)** | **0 (0.0%)** |

Dropping the new metric into the existing thresholds would empty the betting page
permanently. Replacements:

- `GOOD_EDGE` 0.05 → **0.010**
- `EXTREME_EDGE` 0.12 → **0.030**

### Why 0.030 and not 0.020 — read the BANDS, not the cumulative sweep

The cumulative `|edge| >= t` table above is what threshold sweeps normally report, and
it is misleading here: every row contains all the rows below it, so a strong low band
props up every threshold above it. Per-band (disjoint) results on the same 8,233 games:

```
       band     n    win%          95% CI     ROI
0.000-0.005  3024   49.4%  [47.7,51.3]    0.6%
0.005-0.010  2271   47.6%  [45.5,49.7]   -2.4%   <- worst band
0.010-0.015  1588   52.1%  [49.7,54.6]    1.6%
0.015-0.020   757   51.1%  [47.3,54.7]   -0.4%
0.020-0.025   341   53.7%  [48.4,58.9]    3.7%   <- BEST band
0.025-0.030   156   50.0%  [41.7,57.7]   -1.5%
0.030-1.000    96   46.9%  [37.5,57.3]   -6.6%
```

`GOOD_EDGE = 0.010` is well supported: the two bands below it are the weakest (49.4%,
47.6%) and everything above is 50%+.

`EXTREME_EDGE` was **initially set to 0.020 by analogy** with the old moneyline metric,
where a large edge meant overconfidence and the >0.12 bucket lost money — plus a wish to
match the old bar's ~7% selectivity. That reasoning does not survive contact with the
bands: **the 0.020-0.025 band is the best one in the table (53.7%, +3.7% ROI)**, and
excluding it removed 497 value bets for no gain. Corrected to 0.030, which is the only
point where the joint edge actually degrades.

| value-bet band | games | share | win% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.010-0.020 (initial) | 2,345 | 28.5% | 51.8% |
| **0.010-0.030 (shipped)** | **2,842** | **34.5%** | **51.9%** |
| excluded, >= 0.030 | 96 | 1.2% | 46.9% |

Honest caveat in both directions: the 0.030+ cell is n=96 with a CI of [37.5, 57.3].
That is not enough to prove a danger zone exists — CLAUDE.md's power note wants ~400 per
bucket. The guard is kept because the point estimate is the worst in the table and it
costs only 1.2% of games, not because it is established.

## Caveats

- 2026 is the one season where the old edge beats the new one (48.3% vs 47.8%), and
  it is also the smallest fold (n=1,048). Treat the per-season pattern as suggestive.
- The spreads join covers 94.6% of games vs h2h's 96.6% — early-2021 dates where no
  book had posted a run line at the snapshot hour. The research sample starts in 2023
  (walk-forward needs prior folds) so this barely bites here, but it will matter for
  any analysis that reaches back further.
- Second-stage coefficients are refit per season; with 4 features and thousands of
  rows that is stable, but it is still a model fit on top of a model, and its apparent
  calibration advantage should be re-checked once a full live season exists.
