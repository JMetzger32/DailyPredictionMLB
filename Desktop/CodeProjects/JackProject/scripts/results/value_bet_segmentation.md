**The hypothesis is directionally confirmed: the smallest edge bucket is by far the
best.** 5-8% wins 63.9% (+20.2% ROI); everything above 8% collapses to ~52%, barely
above the 52% home-team baseline. A claimed edge above 8pp carries no more predictive
value than a coin flip — exactly the signature of the model disagreeing with the market
hardest where it is least reliable.

**But the picture has shifted since the n=38 sample recorded in CLAUDE.md.** That
earlier snapshot had edge >0.12 at ~30% win / −40% ROI. On the current n=94 the 12%+
bucket is 51.9% / **+5.8% ROI** — mediocre, not catastrophic, and it carries the
highest average CLV of any bucket (+18.9%). The "extreme edges lose money" claim no
longer holds; the defensible claim is the weaker "edge stops discriminating above ~8pp."
Anything currently justified by the strong version of that claim (e.g. excluding
`extreme` from value-bet lists) rests on evidence that has since weakened.

## 2a-bis. The 54.5% headline is partly a labeling artifact, not model behavior

`bet_rating` is computed once when odds attach and never revisited, and the `extreme`
category only came into existence 2026-07-23. Rows rated before that keep
`bet_rating='good'` even when their edge exceeds 0.12 — so the betting page's "Value
Bets" bucket still contains 10 high-edge bets that today's rules would file under
`extreme`.

| segment | n | W-L | win% | avg CLV | net P/L | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as-labeled `good` (**what the page shows**) | 77 | 42-35 | **54.5%** | +6.67% | $+26.04 | +3.4% |
| as-labeled `extreme` | 17 | 11-6 | 64.7% | +22.25% | $+58.81 | +34.6% |
| by edge 0.05 < e ≤ 0.12 (**today's rules**) | 67 | 39-28 | **58.2%** | +5.63% | $+69.10 | +10.3% |
| by edge e > 0.12 (today's rules) | 27 | 14-13 | 51.9% | +18.90% | $+15.75 | +5.8% |

```
10 rows carry bet_rating='good' despite edge >= 0.12
(all dated 2026-07-17 to 2026-07-22, before 'extreme' existed).
```

**This materially changes the framing of the original question.** The reported inversion
was Value Bets 54.5% vs Toss-Ups 59.4% — a 4.9pp gap. Applying the *same* thresholds
consistently to every row, the comparison is **58.2% vs 59.4%: a 1.2pp gap, inside
noise at this n.** Roughly three-quarters of the apparent inversion is a stale-label
artifact of mixing two rating vintages in one bucket, not evidence that the model's
edge signal is inverted.

On ROI — the metric that actually matters for a bettor, since it accounts for price —
consistently-rated value bets return **+10.3% vs Toss-Ups' +3.3%**. Value bets do make
money; they were being scored with a bucket definition that no longer matches the code.

## 2b. Directional skew — the real distortion is home/away, not favorite/underdog

| segment | n | W-L | win% | avg CLV | net P/L | ROI |

```

Favorite/underdog split of ALL resolved rated bets: 181 fav / 41 dog.
```

**Strong home-side skew, and it is the losing side.** Value bets on Home go 51.6% for a
**negative** ROI; value bets on Away go 66.7% for +36.6%. Home picks are also flagged as
value at a similar rate (41% of Home picks vs 47% of Away picks) despite performing far
worse, so the flag is not selecting well on the home side.

> **CORRECTION (2026-08-06, same day):** the paragraph below originally blamed the
> `_HOME_PRIOR = 0.53` blend. **That was wrong.** The blend pulls toward 0.53, which is
> *below* the model's own mean of 0.5432, so it slightly REDUCES home lean. The real
> mechanism is that the model is +1.37pp more home-leaning **than the market** (p=0.027),
> which is what inflates home-side edge. See `scripts/results/clv_and_home_skew.md` §2.

This was initially read as consistent with the `_HOME_PRIOR = 0.53` blend in
`MLBModel.py:940-944` — nudging every probability toward the home team would inflate the
model's home-side probability, inflating home-side edge, and promoting home games into
the value bucket on a bias rather than a signal. The follow-up analysis disproved the
blend as the cause while confirming the home-lean effect itself.
The favorite/underdog split is much weaker (57.6% vs 54.3%) and runs the
opposite way from the P/L (underdogs return more purely because plus-money prices pay
more), so **home/away is the axis that matters, and it points at the home-prior blend
specifically** — supporting the user's Phase 2b hypothesis.

Caveat: 64 vs 30 is not a large sample and the Away bucket's 66.7% is unlikely to hold.
The sign and size of the gap justify investigation, not immediate action.

## Status

- **2a: hypothesis confirmed, with a correction.** Edge is non-monotonic — 5-8% (63.9%)
  hugely outperforms 8-12% (51.6%) and 12%+ (51.9%). But 12%+ is no longer the
  money-loser the older n=38 sample showed (+5.8% ROI now, vs −40% then); CLAUDE.md's
  "extreme edges lose ~40%" fact is **stale and should be softened**.
- **2b: home/away skew confirmed and material** — 64/30 home-tilted flagging, with the
  home side at −3.9% ROI vs away at +36.6%. Cause is the model running +1.37pp more
  home-leaning than the market (p=0.027), **not** the `_HOME_PRIOR` blend — see the
  correction above and `scripts/results/clv_and_home_skew.md` §2.
- **2c: no cluster; the recent tail is 11-4, the best stretch in the sample.** The "last
  three games" complaint is one 1-3 day (08-06), within variance.
- **Biggest finding, unprompted: ~3/4 of the reported inversion is a stale-label
  artifact.** Rating rows consistently gives Value 58.2% vs Toss-Up 59.4% (1.2pp, inside
  noise) rather than 54.5% vs 59.4%. Phase 3 must therefore be judged against the
  *consistent* baseline, not the page's displayed number, or it will appear to fix
  something that was mostly a bookkeeping issue.
